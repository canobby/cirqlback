# CIRQLSPHERE — The Rekindling

*The canonical story & world bible for CIRQL (the CIRQLVERSE flagship), tuned to the cozy-fantasy art we're building with and woven to carry the whole CIRQLSPHERE platform. Supersedes the earlier Lore Bible draft; keeps its bones (light, the Makers, the Grey), sharpens them into a story. Everything here is meant to be **kind, all-ages, whimsical — never preachy or "too real."***

---

## The one line

**The CIRQLSPHERE is a world of light and colour, slowly being *forgotten* into grey. You are a newly-lit spark who sails outward and *relights the islands* — one small saga at a time — until you reach the heart of the grey and, instead of destroying it, *remember* it.**

Wonder vs. forgetting. Colour vs. sameness. And the twist that makes it land: the dark isn't evil — it's *lonely*.

---

# The world — an archipelago of light

The CIRQLSPHERE is a **sea of ring-shaped islands**, rippling outward from the **First Spark** at the center — concentric shores of colour cast like ripples on still water. Each ring is a **raised, luminous island** (a plateau above a dark low sea): a lived-in land with a village at its heart, a **wellspring of light** that feeds a river, and a waterfall that pours that light back over the rim into the sea — the island *giving* its light to the world.

- **CIRQLSPACE (the innermost, ring 0)** is *your* island — your own small spark, yours to build and light.
- **The Town (ring 1)** is the first-relit shore, the friendly hub.
- **The wild islands (rings outward)** grow older, stranger, and **deeper in the grey** the farther you sail.
- **The minimap is the sea-chart:** relit islands glow; islands still lost to the grey sit fogged and unnamed until you reach them.

Sailing between islands is a voyage across the low, shadowed sea — glowing waterfall-islands rising around you, the fog of the grey thinning as each one is relit.

---

# The Light and the Grey

**Light** here isn't just brightness — it's **colour, warmth, song, growth, play, and memory.** It's the stuff the world is made of, and it moves the only way light knows how: **passed hand to hand.** A shared spark, a kindness, a tale told — that's how the world stays lit.

**The Grey** (also *the Unlight*, *the Long Dimming*, *the Bland*) is light's opposite — and crucially, **it is not darkness and not evil.** Darkness can be kindled; the Grey *flattens.* It drains colour to sameness, song to a hum, and — worst of all — it makes places and people **forgotten.** Where it passes, fields go ashen, wellsprings choke, lanterns "forget" how to burn, and creatures wander dull and lost.

> **Blight is only forgotten light.** Return the light, and the ground remembers how to bloom. That single idea is the whole game's verb.

---

# The Everturn — the sign of the circling light

There is one mark older than any village, and it wears no words: a **ring of light that chases its own tail** — two curved arrows circling forever into each other. The folk call it **the Everturn**, and it is the holy sign of the whole CIRQLSPHERE.

Its meaning is the world's first and simplest truth: **light given always comes back around.** When the First Spark passed the first light onward, the light did not leave and vanish — it *circled*, returning warmer for the giving. The Everturn is that promise made into a shape: give a light, and it turns back to you; the circle never truly ends, it only comes round again.

You'll see it **spinning, wordless, above every true CIRQL Fountain** — turning slowly over the wellspring, a small sun of pure sign. Where the Everturn still turns, a place remembers how to give; where the grey has won, it has stopped, gone still and grey like everything else. **To relight a fountain is to set its Everturn turning again.** It is the one emblem the Makers all share, the seal on the Bridge to the real world, and the quiet heart of the game's name — *circling back*.

*(In the world it is the emblem turning above the fountain; it carries no letters, because the sign is older than any word for it.)*

# The Makers

Long ago the First Spark shed six lights of itself — the **Makers** — each an aspect of wonder, who shaped the islands and then, in the event called **the Scattering**, receded into the world they'd made, leaving fragments of themselves behind. Their fingerprints are still on every island; finding them is the lore hunt.

- **Lumen**, the Light-Tender — kindling, lanterns, dawn.
- **Chroma**, the Colour-Weaver — hue, bloom, the dye in a flower.
- **Cadence**, the Song-Keeper — music, festivals, the sound a place makes.
- **Verdant**, the Green Hand — growth, tending, roots, and *creatures*.
- **Mirth**, the Play-Bringer — joy, games, delight for its own sake.
- **Mnemos**, the Memory-Keeper — story, lore, the tale a place refuses to forget.

*(These map cleanly onto our systems: Lumen = the wellsprings/lanterns, Chroma = colour/style, Cadence = music/festivals, Verdant = pets/ecology, Mirth = the arcade, Mnemos = the codex/Oracle.)*

# The villain — the one who was forgotten

Here is the heart of the story, and the reason it's more than "fight the dark."

The Grey has a will behind it, and a face: **the front it wears in the world is BLANDCO** — grey towers, grey rules, a grey promise that everything is *easier* if it's all the same. But BLANDCO is only the mask. Behind it is a **forgotten one** — proposed name **the Ashling** (a seventh spark, or a Kindled left behind in the Scattering) — who could not bear being *forgotten.*

Their logic is heartbreaking and simple: *if no one is special, no one can be left out. If everything is the same and grey, no one is ever forgotten again — because there's nothing left to remember.* The Grey is **loneliness and grief made into a philosophy.** It doesn't hate the world; it's trying, in the most broken way, to make sure the world never hurts the way it did.

**So the ending isn't a battle you win by destroying.** You win by **out-remembering** — by relighting island after island until the world is bright and *full of names* again, and finally by reaching the Ashling and doing the one thing they were sure no one ever would: **remembering them.** Redemption, not annihilation. Kind to the last.

# The player's journey — *The Rekindling*

You wash ashore a new-lit spark and discover you can **carry light.** The world needs exactly that. Your calling is **The Rekindling**: sail outward and bring the lost islands back, one at a time.

**The island-saga loop** (each island is a self-contained little tale, like a *Zelda* region or a *Secret of Mana* chapter):

1. **Arrive** at a half-grey island — a fogged shore, a dimmed village.
2. **Meet the village** — folk drained of colour and cheer, each with a small wound the grey left (a dried spring, a lost lamb, a feud, a fear).
3. **Find the grey's root here** — usually a **corrupted dungeon** or a Grey-touched guardian holding the island's light hostage.
4. **Thread it, and relight** — solve, cleanse (your non-lethal light-combat), and restore the island's **wellspring.**
5. **The island blooms** — colour floods back, the waterfall runs bright, the villagers *remember your name*, and a **signpost points to the next island.** The sea-chart gains a glowing node; the fog retreats a little more.

Threaded through these self-contained sagas is the **through-line mystery**: *Why did the Makers scatter? Where did the First Light's shards go? Who is the Ashling, and can they be reached?* Clues drop into your **codex**; the **Oracle** helps you connect them.

# Escalation — the grey deepens outward

We don't need bizarre biomes to make the outer islands feel wild; we make them feel **wrong.** The farther you sail:

- **Colour drains.** Islands appear as **desaturated, eerie versions** of the cozy world — a grey village, ashen fields, a wellspring clogged with Unlight. (Rendered by *recolouring* the same lovely tiles — creeping wrongness in a place you'd otherwise love, which lands harder than a lava zone.)
- **Dungeons deepen**, the grey-touched grow stranger, and the through-line darkens.
- **The final island** is the **heart of the Long Dimming** — where BLANDCO's grey city stands (that's *Cirql City*), where the shards are hoarded, and where the Ashling waits. The climax is the Rekindling's whole thesis in one act: **relight the one who forgot how to be remembered.**

# How the whole platform lives in one world

The CIRQLSPHERE is the umbrella over everything, and each part has a home in the myth:

- **CIRQLVERSE** — the ring-islands adventure. *The game.*
- **CirqlCade / the arcade** — **Mirth's Hall of Games**: a bright refuge you can step into, full of "the games the Makers played." (The 50+ arcade cabinets, lore-framed.)
- **Cirql City** — the island the grey nearly *won*: a grey **city** you liberate district by district. BLANDCO's stronghold, and the road to the Ashling. (The walkable town we built.)
- **CirqlBreak** — **the origin myth, made playable**: *the night the First Light shattered into a thousand shards.* You knock the shards back together. (The breakout roguelike, as legend.)
- **CIRQLBACK** — **the Bridge.** The one place the story touches the real world, and it touches it *gently and on-theme*: light passed in the *real* world — a kindness, a visit to a neighbourhood shop, a tap — becomes light in the SPHERE. Your real generosity literally helps relight the islands. (The reward bridge already exists; this is its soul, stated as lore — never a sales pitch, just *"light passed hand to hand, in both worlds."*)

# Combat — you don't kill, you *relight*

Fighting fits the genre, but not the killing. So combat is **light-cleansing**, non-lethal:

- Grey-touched creatures (goblins, slimes, skeletons, shroomlings — dull and lost, not evil) block your way.
- You wield **light** — aim, strike, dodge — and instead of dying, a cleansed creature **returns to light**: its colour floods back and it wanders off, freed. (The pack's enemies + a crosshair UI make this real.)
- Mechanically it's satisfying action-combat; thematically it's *restoration.* The light-bearer's weapon is **attention** — you win by refusing to let anything stay forgotten.

# Tone

Warm, mythic, hopeful, with a thread of melancholy-wonder. **Kindness is the mechanic** — no gore, no cruelty; villains are lonely, forgetful, or afraid, and you win by *paying attention.* Codex entries stay short (1–3 sentences); depth comes from *accumulation and connection*, not walls of text.

# One island, start to finish (the meadow)

*You sail up to* **Cloverfield** *— a wavy green shore gone half-grey, its little hamlet dimmed. The* ***wellspring at the village heart has dried***, *its river a dry scar to the cliff. A farmer, colour drained from her cheeks, says her* ***lamb wandered into the old shrine*** *up the hill — and the shrine's gone grey. You follow the dry riverbed up, thread the* ***grey-touched shrine-dungeon***, *and* ***relight its slumped guardian*** *(it wakes, blinks back into colour, and ambles off, free). The* ***wellspring bursts alight***; *light floods the river, the* ***waterfall roars bright over the rim***, *and Cloverfield* ***blooms back into green and gold***. *The farmer knows your name now; the lamb trots home. A* ***signpost by the new bridge points onward***, *and a fresh glowing island appears on your chart. The Oracle notes: "You've relit your first spring. Notice how the grey* recedes *rather than fights? It doesn't want to win. It wants to* stop hurting. *Remember that."*

*That's the loop. That's the game. That's* The Rekindling.
