## Who should review what (before you rely on any of it)

1. **Business attorney** (licensed in `[GOVERNING LAW STATE]`) — Terms of Service, Privacy Policy, Merchant Agreement, Coordinator Contractor Agreement. Especially: limitation of liability, indemnity, arbitration/class-waiver enforceability, auto-renewal disclosure laws, and worker-classification language.
2. **CPA / tax advisor** — coordinator 1099 issuance, sales-tax obligations on subscriptions/add-ons, nexus, and your own entity taxes.
3. **Licensed insurance broker** — the coverages in the safeguards memo; get quotes for General Liability, Tech E&O / Professional Liability, and **Cyber Liability** at minimum.
4. **A privacy specialist** *if* you knowingly serve California residents (CCPA/CPRA), EU/UK residents (GDPR/UK GDPR), or children under 13 (COPPA).

## Highest-priority legal risks to raise with counsel

- **Worker classification.** Coordinators must be genuine independent contractors. Misclassification (treating a contractor like an employee) creates back-tax, penalty, and benefits liability. See the contractor agreement and 1099 guide. If you ever expand to California, Massachusetts, or New Jersey, the strict **"ABC test"** applies and this needs re-review.
- **Payments & PCI.** You use Stripe, which keeps you largely out of direct card-data handling — but confirm you never store raw card numbers and that Stripe Connect payout terms are reflected in the Merchant Agreement.
- **Auto-renewal laws.** Many states require clear pre-checkout disclosure and easy cancellation for auto-renewing subscriptions. Confirm your checkout meets them.
- **Data/privacy law.** Even a small platform can trigger CCPA/CPRA or GDPR depending on who signs up. The Privacy Policy has sections for these — enable/adapt them to reality.
- **Money movement between businesses.** Funded shared-campaign settlements and Stripe Connect payouts should be documented so you are a facilitator, not an unlicensed money transmitter — flag this specifically for counsel.
