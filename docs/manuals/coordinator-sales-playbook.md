# Cirqlback — Coordinator Sales Playbook

**For:** Community Coordinators selling Cirqlback to local businesses.
**Goal:** turn the prospects in your territory into paying businesses — and build recurring income doing it.

> This is your field guide: what to say, to whom, and how to close. Pair it with the **[Coordinator Guide](./coordinator-guide.md)** (how the dashboard works). Everything here is a starting point — the best pitch is the one in your own words, tuned to the shop in front of you.

> **It's built into your dashboard.** Every pitch below is available live: click the **megaphone** on any business in your territory table to get its tailored, name-personalized script and a printable one-pager on the spot. This playbook is the "why" behind that button.

## Why you'll win

You're not cold‑selling software. You're a **local partner** offering a shop owner three things they already want: **more repeat customers**, a **web presence they never got around to building**, and a spot in a **neighborhood network** that sends foot traffic their way. And you can let them **try it free for six months**. That combination opens doors.

The three things to remember:

1. **Lead with the cluster, not the shop.** One "let's put the downtown cafés on a coffee trail" pitch can sign five businesses at once — and each one wants in once their neighbors are in.
2. **Sell the outcome, not the features.** Owners don't buy "NFC tags." They buy "the regular who comes back on a slow Tuesday."
3. **The free trial removes the risk.** Your close is almost always *"the first six months are free — let's set it up right now."*

## The universal pitch arc

Run this in any doorway. Adapt the words; keep the order.

1. **Hook** — a 10‑second question in *their* language that names a problem they feel.
2. **Pain** — name it before they do ("the punch cards get lost," "you don't have a website," "the shop two doors down gets the foot traffic").
3. **Map to their world** — the **2–3 features that matter most for their type** (not all of them).
4. **Picture it** — one concrete scenario using their products and their customers.
5. **Money math** — "it's $20/month; one extra returning customer a week more than covers it."
6. **Objection** — answer the one they raise (see the cheat sheet).
7. **Close** — *"First six months are free. Let's set it up right now."* Onboard them on the spot.

## Business‑type playbooks

Each shop is different. Lead with what lands for *their* type.

### ☕ Café / coffee shop

- **Hook:** *"You know how the paper punch cards end up lost in a drawer? What if the card lived on their phone and pulled them back on a slow morning?"*
- **Their pain:** repeat business is everything, mornings are streaky, and loyalty punch cards get forgotten.
- **Lead with:** the **loyalty punch‑card** (tap goal, e.g. buy‑5‑get‑1), the **hosted page** with today's specials, and — the closer — a **downtown coffee trail** with the other cafés.
- **Picture it:** *"They tap the sticker with each coffee. When they're one away, we can nudge them — 'your free latte's waiting.' That's a Tuesday visit you weren't getting."*
- **Their ROI:** *"It's $20/month. Your average ticket's what, six bucks? One extra return visit a week already pays for it — everything past that is yours."*
- **Top objection — "I already have a rewards app":** *"Does it put you on a shared map and run a trail with the other shops downtown? That's what brings you new faces, not just repeat ones."*
- **Recommended bundle:** **Core + Hosted Page** (Starter trial to begin).
- **Close:** *"Six months free. Let's set your punch‑card up and get a sticker on the counter."*

### 🍽️ Restaurant

- **Hook:** *"What if slow weeknights had their own reward — and you could fill a few more tables without discounting your whole menu?"*
- **Their pain:** thin weeknights, hard to measure what marketing works, no easy way to reward regulars.
- **Lead with:** a **weeknight loyalty/discount campaign**, **tap & redemption analytics** (what actually drives visits), and the **hosted page** as their menu‑and‑hours home.
- **Picture it:** *"A 'tap on your 3rd visit, dessert's on us' card. Regulars chase it; you see exactly how many redeemed it and when."*
- **Their ROI:** *"One extra table a week covers the whole month several times over — and you'll see the numbers to prove it."*
- **Top objection — "I don't have time for another system":** *"You don't run it — customers tap, it tracks itself. You just redeem a code now and then. I'll set the whole thing up for you today."*
- **Recommended bundle:** **Core** (+ **Advanced Analytics** if they're data‑driven).
- **Close:** *"Free for six months. Let's put your weeknight offer live before the dinner rush."*

### 🍺 Bar / brewery / winery

- **Hook:** *"Want to be a must‑stop on a tasting trail that sends groups from tap room to tap room?"*
- **Their pain:** discovery — people don't know they exist; weekend groups follow the crowd.
- **Lead with:** the **cross‑business trail** (the killer feature here), **map discovery**, and **contests/scavenger hunts** for events and releases.
- **Picture it:** *"A 'Yakima Ale Trail' — tap at four spots, earn a prize. Groups make a day of it, and they land on your bar because you're on the map and the trail."*
- **Their ROI:** *"A trail brings in parties, not singles. One group of four on a slow Saturday is your whole month."*
- **Top objection — "We're already busy on weekends":** *"This fills the *weeknights* and the shoulder season — and puts you in front of the tourists who don't know you yet."*
- **Recommended bundle:** **Pro** (for multi‑stop trails and the included hosted page) + **Scavenger‑Hunt Builder** for events.
- **Close:** *"Let's get you on the trail with the others — first six months free. Who else should I bring in?"*

### 💇 Salon / spa

- **Hook:** *"What if the website you keep meaning to build was live this afternoon — with your services, hours, and a rebooking nudge built in?"*
- **Their pain:** no real website, no‑shows and gaps in the book, rebooking is manual.
- **Lead with:** the **hosted page** (their "website they never built"), **reminders** to bring clients back, and **Custom Branding** so it's unmistakably theirs.
- **Picture it:** *"Your `/biz` page with services, hours, and a photo gallery — share it on Instagram. And a 'time for your next visit?' nudge to clients who favorited you."*
- **Their ROI:** *"Your average service is what, $50+? One rebooked client a month covers Core twice over — the page alone is worth it."*
- **Top objection — "My clients aren't techy":** *"There's nothing to download — they tap or click a link. And the page just gives them your info in one place; that part's for everyone."*
- **Recommended bundle:** **Core + Hosted Page + Custom Branding**.
- **Close:** *"Let's build your page right now — free for six months — and get the link on your Instagram today."*

### 🛍️ Retail / boutique

- **Hook:** *"How do people find you versus the shop two doors down? What if you were the highlighted pin on the local map?"*
- **Their pain:** foot‑traffic discovery, competing with nearby shops and online.
- **Lead with:** **Map discovery + Map Priority**, a **loyalty/points** campaign, and a **shopping‑district trail** with neighbors.
- **Picture it:** *"You're the boosted pin when someone browses shops nearby — and part of a 'shop the block' trail that rewards visiting three stores."*
- **Their ROI:** *"A handful of map‑driven walk‑ins a month, average basket $30 — that's the plan paid for and then some."*
- **Top objection — "I sell mostly online":** *"This is your local hook — it drives the in‑person visits and gives you a page to point everyone to. It complements the online side."*
- **Recommended bundle:** **Core + Map Priority** (+ Hosted Page if they lack a site).
- **Close:** *"Let's get you on the map and on the block trail — six months free to prove it out."*

## Objection‑handling cheat sheet (works for any type)

- **"I don't have time."** → *"You don't run it — customers tap, it tracks itself. I set it up for you today; you just redeem a code now and then."*
- **"My customers won't use it."** → *"There's no app to download — they tap a sticker or click a link. And the rewards are the draw; people chase a free coffee."*
- **"It's too expensive."** → *"It's $20 a month, and the first six are free. One extra returning customer usually covers it. If it doesn't earn its keep, you walk."*
- **"I already have loyalty (Square/Toast/a punch card)."** → *"Those reward the regulars you already have. Cirqlback also brings *new* faces — the map and the shared trails with nearby shops. That's the part they can't do."*
- **"I tried something like this before."** → *"What went wrong? Usually it's setup and adoption — I handle the setup, and there's nothing to download. Try it free for six months and judge for yourself."*
- **"Let me think about it."** → *"Totally fair — and since it's free for six months, there's nothing to think about on cost. Let's turn it on now; you can always stop. What's the worst that happens?"*

## Your force multiplier: the cross‑business trail

Don't sell one shop at a time. Sell a **cluster**:

1. Pick a **theme + area** — "downtown coffee trail," "Yakima ale trail," "shop‑the‑block."
2. Line up an **anchor** (the most respected shop) first — others follow a name they trust.
3. Pitch each neighbor: *"You're on the trail with [anchor] and the others — tap at all of them, customers earn a prize, and you send each other traffic."*
4. Launch the trail from your dashboard and **feature it on the map**.

One trail = several sign‑ups, obvious mutual value, and lock‑in (no shop wants to be the one *not* on it).

## Your first 30 / 60 / 90 days

- **Days 1–30 — Anchors.** Land 2–3 respected anchor businesses. Get each fully set up and winning. Their word‑of‑mouth is your best salesperson.
- **Days 31–60 — Cluster & trail.** Sign the neighbors around each anchor. Launch your first **trail** and feature it on the map — now the network effect sells for you.
- **Days 61–90 — Upgrade & compound.** Move happy shops to **Pro**, add the **Hosted Page** and other add‑ons, and start annual plans. Your recurring number climbs while you open the next cluster.

Work your prospect list by **anchor + cluster**, not alphabetically. Momentum is everything.
